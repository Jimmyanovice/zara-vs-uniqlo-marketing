# B – Marketing Strategy Analysis Outline
**Focus**: 3 core strategy differences between UNIQLO and ZARA (4Ps framework)
**Date**: 21 October 2026

---

## Preliminary Judgement 1: Two distinct product growth logics for mass market penetration
### Core Conclusion
UNIQLO captures the mass market through product specialization with long-lifecycle basic items, while ZARA achieves scale through trend-led market generalization with short-lifecycle fast fashion products.

### Brand Evidence & Sources
- **UNIQLO**: Over 80% of SKUs are basic functional apparel with a 6–12 month lifecycle; core lines (HEATTECH, AIRism) remain stable across seasons.
  Source: UNIQLO China official website, published Sep 2025, accessed 21 Oct 2026
- **ZARA**: Over 10,000 new SKUs launched annually, with an average product lifecycle of 2–4 weeks; weekly new arrivals maintain trend freshness.
  Source: ZARA official website & apparel industry reports, 2025, accessed 21 Oct 2026

### Supporting Theories & Literature
- Big Middle hypothesis: two pathways to mass market dominance (product specialization vs market generalization)
- Reference: Azuma, N., Yokoyama, N., & Kim, W. (2022). Revisiting the Big Middle: an fsQCA approach.

### Limitations, Counterexamples & Missing Data
- Limitation: Evidence is limited to the Chinese market; product strategies may vary across regional markets
- Missing data: Official SKU volume and exact product lifecycle data from brand annual reports
- Counterexample: UNIQLO’s designer collaboration lines partially overlap with ZARA’s trend-focused positioning

## Preliminary Judgement 2: Contrasting pricing models shape different consumer price perceptions
### Core Conclusion
UNIQLO follows an EDLP-like pricing pattern with stable prices and infrequent promotions, while ZARA adopts a Hi-Lo pricing strategy with premium new arrivals and deep end-of-season discounts.

### Brand Evidence & Sources
- **UNIQLO**: Stable daily prices with only 2 major seasonal sales per year; discount intensity stays below 30% for most regular items.
  Source: UNIQLO China 2026 promotion calendar, accessed 21 Oct 2026
- **ZARA**: Full-price policy for new collections; 50–70% off during mid-year and year-end clearance events.
  Source: ZARA China official mid-year sale page, Jun 2026, accessed 21 Oct 2026

### Supporting Theories & Literature
- Two dominant retail pricing frameworks: EDLP vs Hi-Lo pricing strategy
- Reference price theory and consumer waiting behaviour
- Reference: Anderson, E. T., & Fox, E. J. (2019). How price promotions work: A review of practice and theory.

### Limitations, Counterexamples & Missing Data
- Limitation: Pricing comparison does not distinguish between product categories (basic items vs trend items)
- Missing data: Specific discount depth statistics and price elasticity metrics for both brands
- Counterexample: ZARA’s permanent basic wardrobe line also maintains stable pricing, similar to UNIQLO’s pattern

## Preliminary Judgement 3: Significant gaps in omnichannel integration and cross-channel experience coherence
### Core Conclusion
UNIQLO operates a highly integrated omnichannel model with full functional alignment, while ZARA adopts a store-centric model with weaker digital-physical sensory and service coherence.

### Brand Evidence & Sources
- **UNIQLO**: Unified inventory, pricing and membership across online and offline channels; supports in-store pickup, return and exchange for online orders.
  Source: UNIQLO official help center, Jan 2026, accessed 21 Oct 2026
- **ZARA**: Prime-location flagship stores as core brand touchpoints; online channel has limited inventory synchronization and fewer cross-channel services.
  Source: ZARA official website & in-store observation, 2026, accessed 21 Oct 2026

### Supporting Theories & Literature
- Omnichannel sensory coherence and Expectancy-Disconfirmation Theory
- Digital stimuli’s impact on in-store experiential value
- Reference: Cenizo, C. (2026). From online to offline: web-based brand stimuli in omnichannel fashion retail.

### Limitations, Counterexamples & Missing Data
- Limitation: Focuses on functional integration, lacks measurement of actual consumer experience outcomes
- Missing data: Omnichannel conversion rate and cross-channel customer satisfaction data for both brands
- Counterexample: ZARA has upgraded its app and online services in recent years, narrowing the integration gap
