## 2. 主要理论

综述服装生产、使用和废弃的环境负担，强调减缓消耗、延长使用寿命及系统性改变，不能仅以回收承诺替代整体评价。

## 3. 比较用途

同时审视两品牌的材料、使用寿命和循环服务，而非先验认定基础款天然绿色、快时尚必然违法。品牌级判断须有供应链和使用数据。

## 4. 适用章节

长期竞争压力及循环服务备选建议。

## 5. 局限

全球行业综述不测量中国顾客付费、品牌声誉或单店盈利。历史行业估计不能当作当前品牌排放；修补件数也不足以证明净减排。

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s43017-020-0039-9

### Key Theory & Findings
Niinimäki and an international team of textile and environmental scientists provide a comprehensive, multi-dimensional environmental assessment of the global fast-fashion system:
1. **Systemic Environmental Footprint**: The global textile and apparel industry accounts for 8–10% of global carbon emissions (surpassing international aviation and shipping combined), consumes 79 trillion litres of water annually, and produces over 92 million tonnes of textile waste each year (pp. 189–191).
2. **The Structural Driver of Degradation**: Identifies the fundamental root cause as the *accelerated throughput business model*—a linear economic paradigm that commodifies trend obsolescence, resulting in garment utilization declining by more than 36% between 2000 and 2015 while total garment production doubled (p. 192).
3. **The Imperative for Circularity & Longevity**: Highlights that technological recycling alone cannot solve the crisis due to composite fabric blending and microplastic shedding (synthetic fibers release ~0.5 million tonnes of microfibers annually). The only viable long-term mitigation lies in **slowing down consumption cycles**, extending active garment lifespan, and establishing closed-loop circular systems (repair, redesign, resale) (pp. 194–197).

### Value for UNIQLO vs ZARA Comparison
This paper serves as the essential theoretical foundation for evaluating environmental vulnerability and long-term strategic legitimacy:
- **Diagnosing ZARA's Existential Vulnerability**: ZARA epitomises the high-velocity, short-lifecycle model critiqued by the authors. Although Inditex has published ambitious sustainability goals (e.g. 100% preferred or recycled materials), its core business architecture remains bound to planned trend obsolescence, exposing it to escalating regulatory risks (e.g. EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence, Extended Producer Responsibility waste fees, and French fast-fashion penalties) and reputational backlash among educated urban youths.
- **Assessing UNIQLO's Sustainability Strategic Dilemma**: UNIQLO's focus on long-lifecycle, timeless basic apparel ("Made for All", "LifeWear") inherently aligns better with garment longevity and slow consumption. However, UNIQLO relies heavily on synthetic petroleum-derived fibers (polyester, acrylic, nylon in fleece and HEATTECH), making it vulnerable to the microplastics critique documented in this paper.
- **Supporting Sustainable Competitive Advantage**: Proves that sustainability is no longer merely a PR exercise but a decisive determinant of strategic survival and brand equity. UNIQLO's existing investments in "Re.UNIQLO" (in-store garment collection, repair studios, down-to-down recycling) provide a rare structural asset to convert environmental pressures into a tangible brand differentiator.

### Research Limitations
- The paper focuses primarily on environmental sciences and life-cycle assessments (LCA), offering limited empirical insight into consumer willingness to pay a price premium for circular services in emerging markets.
- Macro-level global data does not disaggregate the regulatory variations between Western European jurisdictions and the Chinese mainland market.
- Does not account for brand-level marketing narratives and how brands mitigate consumer guilt through promotional framing.
