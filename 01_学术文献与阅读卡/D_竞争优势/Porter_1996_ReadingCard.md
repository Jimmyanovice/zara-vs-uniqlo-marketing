## 2. 主要理论

运营改善并不等于独特战略；取舍和活动间契合可用于判断建议是否与品牌定位相容。

## 3. 比较用途

先比较两品牌价值强调，再问商品、传播与服务是否互相支持。该框架不证明优衣库不能做设计款，也不证明 ZARA 不能提供基础衣橱；这些判断需具体品牌资料。

## 4. 适用章节

## 5. 局限

### Key Theory & Findings
Porter establishes the fundamental boundary between **Operational Effectiveness (OE)** and **Strategic Positioning**:
1. **OE vs. Strategy**: Operational effectiveness means performing similar activities better than rivals (e.g. benchmarking, TQM, rapid prototyping). While necessary, OE easily diffuses and leads to competitive convergence, price wars, and zero-sum competition (pp. 62–64). In contrast, strategy is the creation of a unique and valuable position, involving a set of activities that are deliberately different from rivals'.
2. **Strategic Trade-offs**: Competitive advantage requires explicit trade-offs—choosing what *not* to do (p. 68). Trade-offs arise from three sources: inconsistencies in brand image/reputation, differences in physical activities, and limits on organizational coordination. A sustainable position cannot be achieved without trade-offs, as "straddling" destroys focus and dilutes core value.
3. **Activity System Fit**: Strategy is about combining activities into an integrated, mutually reinforcing system ("Fit"), making competitive advantage resilient against imitators (pp. 70–73).

### Value for UNIQLO vs ZARA Comparison
This seminal paper provides crucial conceptual discipline for diagnosing competitive advantage and framing strategic recommendations:
- **Explaining Strategic Differentiation**: UNIQLO and ZARA demonstrate distinct, highly coherent activity systems:
  - *UNIQLO's System*: Standardised product development + huge production runs + material science R&D + clean, self-service warehouse-style retail layouts. Every activity reinforces cost efficiency, functional consistency, and broad demographic appeal.
  - *ZARA's System*: Small batch runs + short capital-intensive European assembly + prime metropolitan flagship visual merchandising + rapid trend copying. Every activity reinforces fashion immediacy and exclusivity.
- **Enforcing Trade-offs Against "Straddling"**: Critical for formulating recommendations. UNIQLO should **not** attempt to become a fast-fashion trend chaser (e.g., launching thousands of micro-trend styles), as that would shatter its economies of scale and Toray procurement contracts. Similarly, ZARA cannot easily implement long-lifecycle basic clothing without undermining its fashion-forward image and high inventory turnover rhythm.
- Recommendations must preserve the integrity of the focal brand's activity system rather than proposing generic operational improvements that compromise strategic trade-offs.

### Applicable Report Sections
- Chapter 1 & 2: Introduction & Overview of Strategic Positioning
- Chapter 3: Theoretical Framework (Competitive Strategy and Trade-off Theory)
- Chapter 5: Strategic Positioning & Activity System Comparison
- Chapter 6: Recommendations for Competitive Advantage (justifying boundaries and ensuring non-straddling solutions)

### Research Limitations
- The paper presents a conceptual, managerial framework rather than a quantified empirical model.
- Primarily assumes clear industrial boundaries and stable competitive structures; may underestimate digital platform disruptions, modular supply networks, and hybrid mass-customization models.
- Provides limited operational guidance on how incumbent firms navigate regional demand shocks or implement incremental organizational experiments.
