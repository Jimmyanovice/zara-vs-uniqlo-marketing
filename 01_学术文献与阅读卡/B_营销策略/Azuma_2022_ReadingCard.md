# Reading Card: Azuma et al. (2022)
## Full Reference (APA 7th Edition)
Azuma, N., Yokoyama, N., & Kim, W. (2022). Revisiting the Big Middle: an fsQCA approach to unpack a large value market from a product specialist retailer’s perspective. *International Journal of Retail & Distribution Management*, 50(8/9), 900–921. https://doi.org/10.1108/IJRDM-10-2021-0496

## Key Theory & Findings
Grounded in the Big Middle retail evolution hypothesis and aligned with the **market orientation** concept from the course, this fsQCA study of UNIQLO demonstrates that product specialist retailers can capture the mass market by aggregating heterogeneous consumer segments through a standardized **marketing mix (4Ps)** framework (p. 900). It identifies convenient store location and personnel-environment fit as necessary conditions for customer patronage, while price promotion attractiveness acts as a core driver across most segments (p. 911). The findings align with **low-involvement consumer decision-making** patterns, where convenience and functional value are dominant choice criteria for casual apparel purchases.

## Value for UNIQLO vs ZARA Comparison
This paper frames the two brands’ distinct growth logics within competitive strategy theory: UNIQLO follows a **cost leadership + product specialization** path with long-lifecycle basic items, while ZARA adopts a **differentiation-led fast fashion** path with trend-driven short-lifecycle products. It also supports 4Ps comparison in store location strategy, pricing-promotion patterns, and target segment divergence, linking to differences in consumer choice criteria (functional/economic vs social/style).

## Applicable Report Sections
Chapter 3 (Theoretical Framework: marketing concept, competitive strategy); Chapter 4 (Product, Price, Place strategy comparison); Chapter 5 (Competitive positioning & consumer behaviour analysis).

## Research Limitations
Findings are not statistically generalizable (p. 915); data is limited to Japan’s 2015 offline retail context, excluding omnichannel operations and social media’s impact on consumer behaviour. Only the product-specialist pathway is verified, not the fast-fashion generalization route.
