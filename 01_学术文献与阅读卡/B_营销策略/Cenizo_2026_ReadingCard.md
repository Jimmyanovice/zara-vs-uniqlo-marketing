### Full Reference (APA 7th Edition)
Cenizo, C. (2026). From online to offline: how web-based brand stimuli shape the physical store experience in omnichannel fashion retail. *Journal of Fashion Marketing and Management: An International Journal*, 30(4), 625–649.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1108/JFMM-07-2025-0329

### Key Theory & Findings
Grounded in **Expectancy-Disconfirmation Theory** and omnichannel consumer behaviour frameworks from the course, this study examines how digital personalization and sensory brand experience affect in-store experiential value in fashion retail (p. 626). Key findings: (1) digital personalization *reduces* in-store experiential value when lacking sensory coherence across channels (p. 639); (2) consistent online sensory brand experience significantly enhances offline value perceptions; (3) consumer sensory orientation moderates both effects, with high-sensitivity consumers responding more strongly to cross-channel coherence.

### Value for UNIQLO vs ZARA Comparison
This paper supports 4Ps comparison in **place (omnichannel)** and **promotion (digital experience)**:
- UNIQLO’s highly integrated omnichannel system (unified pricing, inventory, and visual identity online/offline) delivers strong sensory coherence, matching the positive effect of cross-channel sensory alignment.
- ZARA’s store-centric model features strong in-store sensory experience but weaker digital-physical alignment, illustrating the risk of experiential dissonance when online touchpoints lack sensory continuity.

### Research Limitations
- Quasi-experimental cross-sectional design cannot confirm causal relationships (p. 643)
- Sample does not segment by brand type (fast fashion vs luxury), limiting direct generalisability to UNIQLO/ZARA
- Relies on self-reported data, prone to recall and social desirability bias
- Only covers sensory and experiential dimensions, excluding functional omnichannel elements such as logistics and inventory integration
