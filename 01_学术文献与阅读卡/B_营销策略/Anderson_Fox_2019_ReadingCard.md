### Full Reference (APA 7th Edition)
Anderson, E. T., & Fox, E. J. (2019). How price promotions work: A review of practice and theory (Chapter 9). In *Handbook of the Economics of Marketing*. North-Holland.  
Source: Emerald Academic Database

---

### Key Theory & Findings
Aligned with the **marketing mix (4Ps)** framework and economic choice criteria in consumer behaviour from the course, this chapter systematically reviews two dominant retail pricing patterns: **EDLP (Everyday Low Price)** and **Hi-Lo pricing** (p. 342). It concludes that EDLP fosters stable price trust and reduces consumer waiting behaviour, while Hi-Lo pricing leverages periodic promotions to drive short-term traffic and clear inventory. It also applies reference price theory to explain how pricing strategies shape consumers’ purchase intentions.

### Value for UNIQLO vs ZARA Comparison
This paper provides the theoretical foundation for **price strategy comparison** in the 4Ps analysis:
- UNIQLO adopts an EDLP-like approach with stable everyday prices and infrequent promotions, building long-term price credibility and reducing decision friction for low-involvement casual apparel purchases.
- ZARA follows a typical Hi-Lo strategy: premium pricing for new trend items and deep end-of-season discounts, supporting rapid inventory turnover and capturing consumers’ willingness to pay for novelty.

### Applicable Report Sections
- Chapter 4: Price strategy comparison in the 4Ps analysis
- Chapter 5: Consumer behaviour analysis (price perception and purchase decision patterns)

### Research Limitations
- Focuses on general retail economics and lacks fast-fashion-specific empirical evidence
- Findings are mainly based on the US market, with limited adaptability to Asian consumer price sensitivity
- Does not cover dynamic pricing or personalised digital promotion models in e-commerce
